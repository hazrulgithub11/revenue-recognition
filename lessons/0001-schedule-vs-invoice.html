Lesson 0001 · RTO billing design

What real systems do: schedule ≠ invoice

For rent-to-own, you will create one Amortisation Period per month, then later raise a Books Invoice from that period. That two-layer pattern is what loan/ERP systems do — not what pure SaaS billing usually does.

The real-world pattern (loan / installment ERPs)

Loan and installment products (Odoo loan apps, ERPNext lending, SAP installment plans) almost always keep three layers:

  1. Contract / loan master — term, principal, rate, customer.
  2. Installment / amortisation schedule — one child row per due period (opening, interest, principal, closing, due date, status).
  3. Invoice (or receivable document) — created from a schedule row when that period is billable; payment clears the invoice and flips the row to Paid.

See Odoo LoanSuite’s flow: compute installments on the loan, then generate installment invoices (bulk or by scheduler), each tied back to the loan — jupical.io loan management. SAP treats an installment plan as a schedule of future receivables used for collection — SAP Learning.

Contract (RTO) │ ├── Amortisation Period × 36 ← your RTO Transactions │ │ │ └── Invoice (when due) ← Zoho Books Invoice │ │ │ └── Payment received │ └── (journals can aggregate periods / FY)

Why not “just invoices”?

An invoice answers: what does the customer owe this period? (cash = RM399). Your Excel schedule also answers: how much of that is interest vs principal, and what is the remaining receivable? Those numbers must live on the schedule row before and after billing — otherwise failed payments, partials, or early buyout corrupt the income statement.

BillingPlatform and SaaS billing architecture both stress: billing and recognition answer different questions; collapsing them into one table fails the first time payment and earning diverge — BillingPlatform, SaaS Billing Architecture.

What SaaS (Stripe / Chargebee) does differently

Subscriptions often do not pre-create 36 amort rows. They keep a subscription with next_billing_at and spawn an Invoice each cycle. That works when the charge is “plan price” and recognition is a separate waterfall — not when every period needs reducing-balance interest/principal like a finance lease.

Stripe billing data model: Subscription → Invoice (recurring), not a stored EMI amort table.

GRSB RTO is closer to a loan schedule than to Stripe. Pre-creating 36 Amortisation Periods on contract create is the right industry analogue.

Your Zoho design (aligned with industry)

  • On contract create → create 36 RTO Transaction records (Amortisation Periods).
  • View them on the contract via related list (cleaner screen — your Q5 goal).
  • Later: for period N due → create Books Invoice for Payment Amount; store invoice/payment refs on the period; Status → Invoiced → Paid.
Do not copy the 36 rows into a Table on the contract as a second store. Related list is the nested view. A Table would duplicate truth and break “invoice from this period.” Books Tables are fine for other line-item use cases — custom module tables.
